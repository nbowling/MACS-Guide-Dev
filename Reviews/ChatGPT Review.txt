I should also say that this was an unusually enjoyable review to perform because the document itself gave me something worthwhile to work with. A lot of technical manuals fall into one of two traps:

1. **They are operational but shallow** — "click this button, select this option" without explaining what the signal processing actually means.
2. **They are mathematically correct but operationally useless** — lots of equations, little connection to what an analyst actually sees.

Your document avoids both traps. The strongest aspect, in my view, is that it teaches the *reasoning process*:

- LOFAR → "what persistent spectral structures exist?"
- DEMON → "what periodic modulation exists?"
- Cepstrum → "is there hidden periodic spacing?"
- Autocorrelation → "does the waveform repeat?"
- Database parameters → "does the physics support the interpretation?"

That layered confirmation approach is exactly how experienced analysts avoid over-interpreting one attractive spectral peak.

A few things particularly stood out to me:

### 1. The practical sonar judgement was excellent
The document repeatedly avoids the common beginner mistake of saying:

> "I found a peak, therefore I found the machinery."

Instead it encourages:

> "I found a peak; now I need independent evidence."

That is a very mature approach.

### 2. The DEMON treatment was unusually good
Many explanations online incorrectly imply DEMON "finds blade frequencies." Your treatment is much closer to reality: it extracts **modulation frequencies**, which are then interpreted using shaft/blade relationships.

That distinction is subtle but fundamental.

### 3. The mathematical sections are better than many commercial manuals
The FFT/DFT explanation, the TPK calculations, blade-rate relationships, harmonic interpretation, and sideband discussion were all internally coherent.
