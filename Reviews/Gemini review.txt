## 2. Executive Summary

The *MACS User Guide and Technical Reference (v0.2.5)* by Nick Bowling (@Skittles) and technical advisors (@mahzel, @snrkatat) is a comprehensive, well-structured community document supporting the **Modern Naval Warfare (MNW)** naval simulation. It bridges the gap between raw acoustic digital signal processing (DSP) theory and practical in-game passive sonar analysis.

The guide covers software installation across Windows, macOS, and Linux, database management, foundational underwater acoustics, advanced analysis techniques (LOFAR, DEMON, Cepstrum, ACF, PSD, ZoomFFT), step-by-step classification workflows, and rigorous mathematical appendices.

While the technical foundation is robust and reflects genuine passive sonar principles, the document contains several typographical errors in formulas, minor internal inconsistencies, and sections marked as Work In Progress (WIP) that require technical refinement.

## 4. Independent Verification of Worked Calculations

All major worked calculations in the text were independently verified for mathematical rigor and dimensional consistency:

### 1. Turns Per Knot (TPK) Formula (Page 17)

$$TPK = \frac{SR \times 60}{Speed}$$

* **Verification:** Shaft Rate ($SR$) in rev/s ($\text{Hz}$) multiplied by $60$ yields revolutions per minute ($\text{RPM}$). Dividing by speed in knots gives shaft revolutions per nautical mile-per-hour equivalent. **Dimensionally consistent.**



### 2. Engine Cylinder Count Calculation (Pages 32–33)

* **Given:** Observed EFR = $31.25\text{ Hz}$, Engine Speed $N = 750\text{ RPM}$, 4-stroke engine.
* **Step 1 (Firings per minute):** $31.25\text{ Hz} \times 60\text{ s/min} = 1875\text{ firings/min}$. **Correct.**


* **Step 2 (4-stroke relation):** For a 4-stroke engine, each cylinder fires once every 2 revolutions. Total firings per minute = $\frac{N}{2} \times C$, where $C$ is cylinder count.

$$\frac{750}{2} \times C = 375 \times C$$


$$1875 = 375 \times C \implies C = \frac{1875}{375} = 5 \text{ cylinders}$$


 **Mathematically verified.**


* **Alternative (2-stroke rejection):** For a 2-stroke engine, $1875 = 750 \times C \implies C = 2.5$. Since fractional cylinders are physically impossible, the 2-stroke hypothesis is correctly rejected.



### 3. Induction Motor Synchronous Speed (Pages 33–34)

$$N_s = \frac{120f}{P}$$

* **Given:** $f = 60\text{ Hz}$, $P = 4$ poles.
* **Calculation:**

$$N_s = \frac{120 \times 60}{4} = \frac{7200}{4} = 1800\text{ RPM}$$


 **Correct.**


* **Rotational Frequency:**

$$f_r = \frac{1800}{60} = 30\text{ Hz}$$


 **Correct.**



### 4. Sliding-Window Z-Score Normalization Example (Page 48)

* **Given window values:** $95, 102, 98, 105, 140$ (centered on Bin 3, value $98$).
* **Mean ($\mu$):** $\frac{95 + 102 + 98 + 105 + 140}{5} = \frac{540}{5} = 108$. **Correct.**


* **Standard Deviation ($\sigma$):** Text states $\sigma = 16$. (Sample/population variance calculations yield $\approx 16.36$ to $18.29$, making $16$ a reasonable working approximation).


* **Z-Score for center bin ($98$):**

$$z_3 = \frac{98 - 108}{16} = \frac{-10}{16} = -0.625 \approx -0.63$$


 **Correct.**



### 5. Speed through Water with Propeller Slip (Pages 54–55)

* **Given:** $SR = 5\text{ Hz}$ ($300\text{ RPM}$), Pitch $P = 8\text{ ft/rev}$, Slip $S = 15\%$ ($0.15$, efficiency factor $= 0.85$).
* **Theoretical Speed:**

$$\text{Distance/min} = 300\text{ rev/min} \times 8\text{ ft/rev} = 2400\text{ ft/min}$$


$$\text{Distance/hr} = 2400 \times 60 = 144,000\text{ ft/hr}$$


$$\text{Knots} = \frac{144,000}{6076\text{ ft/nmi}} \approx 23.699 \text{ kts}$$


 **Correct.**


* **Speed with Slip:**

$$\text{Actual Speed} = 23.7 \times (1 - 0.15) = 23.7 \times 0.85 \approx 20.1\text{ Knots}$$


 **Correct numerical result.** *(Note: Text has a typographical slip in the formula representation, discussed below).*

## 5. Categorized Review Findings

### Technical Errors

1. **Slip Formula Typographical Error (Appendix B, Page 55):**
* *Issue:* In Section 11.2.6, the text writes: `23.7 * (1 - 0.85) = 23.7 * 0.15 ≈ 20.1 Knots/hr`.


* *Correction:* Since slip is $15\%$, the efficiency multiplier is $(1 - 0.15) = 0.85$, not $0.85$ slip loss. The formula should read `23.7 * (1 - 0.15) = 23.7 * 0.85 ≈ 20.1 Knots`. The final number is correct, but the expression inside the parentheses is mathematically inconsistent with a $15\%$ slip value.



### Statements Requiring Qualification

1. **DEMON Peak Dominance (Section 6.5, Page 28):**
* *Statement:* *"The most visible peak on DEMON is quite often that of the BR and not the SR..."*


* *Qualification:* While blade rate modulation is frequently prominent due to hydrodynamic wake loading and blade-to-blade asymmetries, shaft rate (SR) remains the fundamental mechanical rotation frequency. Analysts should be cautioned that dominant DEMON peaks depend heavily on cavitation intensity, transducer orientation, and hull wake characteristics.


2. **Electrical Mains Frequency Standards (Section 6.9, Page 30):**
* *Statement:* Links NATO naval electrical standards strictly to $60\text{ Hz}$ via ANEP-100.


* *Qualification:* While many modern blue-water NATO navies utilize $60\text{ Hz}$ systems (e.g., USN, RN, RCN), several European NATO navies (including historical and specific auxiliary classes) operate $50\text{ Hz}$ or mixed frequency systems. This should be presented as a general tendency rather than an absolute rule.



### Internal Inconsistencies

1. **WIP Markings and Placeholder Sections:**
* *Issue:* Several key sections (e.g., Advanced Tonal Editor, Overlays, Contact Matching, Noise Subtraction, ZoomFFT, and Appendices on Contact Database Structure) are marked as `*** WIP ***` or left blank.


* *Impact:* While expected in a living community document, users navigating to advanced tools will encounter incomplete documentation.





### Suggested Improvements

1. **Clarify Z-Score Window Parameters:** In Appendix B (Section 11.1.3), expand the practical guidance on selecting sliding-window half-widths (`Mean Width` vs. `StdDev Width`) with concrete examples of how highly non-stationary noise floors impact $\sigma > 2\mu$ ratios.


2. **Unified Notation for Shaft and Blade Rates:** Standardize nomenclature across sections. The text occasionally alternates between $SR$, $SF$, $BR$, and $BPF$ without immediate cross-referencing, which can overwhelm novice analysts.


3. **Complete WIP Sections:** Prioritize filling out the `Noise Subtraction` and `ZoomFFT` operational guides, as these are critical features for advanced signal filtering in noisy acoustic environments.
