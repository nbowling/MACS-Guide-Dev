Worked Analysis Synopsis: LOFAR, DEMON and Cepstrum
From Acoustic Observation to Propulsion Evidence

This exercise demonstrates how a relatively complicated acoustic signal can be examined through three complementary forms of analysis: LOFAR, DEMON and cepstrum. Each technique approaches the signal differently. More importantly, the observations can be compared with one another and with the mathematical relationships expected from rotating machinery.

The synthetic example represents a five-bladed propeller turning at a Shaft Rate (SR) of 1.30 Hz, equivalent to 78 RPM. Its corresponding Blade Rate (BR) is therefore 6.50 Hz, with further related structure at multiples such as 13.00 Hz.

The objective is not simply to find those numbers in a graph. The exercise demonstrates the reasoning process by which several apparently different observations may be recognised as manifestations of the same underlying mechanical process.

Image 1: The Waveform

The first image shows the acoustic waveform in the time domain.

At this stage, the complete signal is represented as amplitude against time. Although the waveform confirms the presence and evolution of acoustic energy, most of the useful mechanical relationships are difficult to determine directly by eye.

This is an important starting point. Information can be present in a signal without being visually obvious in the time waveform.

The remaining analyses therefore transform or process the same underlying information to make particular characteristics easier to observe.

Image 2: The LOFAR Spectrogram

The second image introduces frequency as a major analytical dimension.

The LOFAR-style display shows frequency against time, with intensity indicating the relative strength of spectral components. Persistent tonal components consequently appear differently from broadband energy or components that vary with time.

LOFAR asks:

What frequency components are present, and how persistent are they?

This is the first move from observing a complex acoustic waveform towards identifying structure within it.

The synthetic signal contains machinery-related frequencies and modulated carrier energy. LOFAR therefore provides evidence of the signal's spectral organisation. It can show tonals, harmonics and potentially the effects of modulation around stronger components.

However, the presence of a frequency line does not by itself establish what produced it. The MACS review specifically identifies this distinction as important: a tonal peak is not automatically machinery.

LOFAR therefore supplies the spectral evidence, but interpretation requires further corroboration.

Image 3: The Mean LOFAR Spectrum

The third image collapses the time dimension by averaging the LOFAR spectra.

The result makes persistent frequencies easier to inspect quantitatively.

The distinction between Images 2 and 3 is useful:

the LOFAR spectrogram answers when and where spectral energy occurs;
the mean spectrum emphasises which spectral components persist strongly across the observation.

A peak in the mean spectrum is consequently an analytical clue rather than an automatic identification.

Relationships between peaks matter considerably more.

If components show harmonic spacing, sideband relationships or other regular structure, the analyst has a basis for asking whether they share a common mechanical origin.

This naturally leads to DEMON analysis.

Image 4: The DEMONgram

DEMON provides a fundamentally different view of the signal.

Rather than simply asking which acoustic frequencies are present, DEMON examines periodic amplitude modulation within a selected acoustic band. This distinction is fundamental and was specifically highlighted as a strength in the review of the MACS material: DEMON extracts modulation frequencies, which are subsequently interpreted through shaft and blade relationships.

The DEMONgram therefore asks:

At what rates is the acoustic energy being modulated, and how do those rates behave with time?

That question is particularly relevant to rotating machinery.

In the synthetic example there is a known Shaft Rate:

SR=1.30 HzSR=1.30\text{ Hz}

and a five-bladed propeller.

The corresponding Blade Rate is therefore:

BR=B×SRBR=B\times SR

where BB is the number of blades.

Thus:

BR=5×1.30=6.50 HzBR=5\times1.30=6.50\text{ Hz}

The DEMON display allows those low-frequency modulation relationships to become much more apparent than they may have been in the original waveform or conventional spectral display.

Image 5: The Mean DEMON Spectrum

The fifth image is particularly useful for interpretation because it converts the DEMONgram into an averaged modulation spectrum.

The principal frequencies of interest are:

1.30 Hz,6.50 Hz,13.00 Hz1.30\text{ Hz},\quad6.50\text{ Hz},\quad13.00\text{ Hz}

These are not three arbitrary numbers.

They form a mathematically related family.

The 1.30 Hz component corresponds to the known Shaft Rate, while:

6.501.30=5\frac{6.50}{1.30}=5

This is consistent with a five-bladed propeller.

Furthermore:

13.00=2×6.5013.00=2\times6.50

so 13.00 Hz is consistent with the second harmonic of Blade Rate.

The value of the DEMON spectrum therefore lies not merely in finding a strong peak, but in recognising a coherent pattern:

SR→BR→2BRSR\rightarrow BR\rightarrow2BR

That provides substantially more meaningful evidence than interpreting any one component alone.

Image 6: The Real Cepstrum

The sixth image examines the signal from yet another perspective.

Cepstral analysis is useful for revealing regular spectral spacing. Its horizontal coordinate is termed quefrency and has units of time.

The basic relationship is:

q=1Δfq=\frac{1}{\Delta f}

where Δf\Delta f is a repeating frequency spacing.

For the 6.50 Hz Blade Rate relationship:

q=16.50≈0.154 sq=\frac{1}{6.50}\approx0.154\text{ s}

For the 1.30 Hz shaft relationship:

q=11.30≈0.769 sq=\frac{1}{1.30}\approx0.769\text{ s}

The significance of the cepstrum is therefore that it provides another way of searching for periodic organisation within the signal.

LOFAR presents spectral structure.

DEMON presents modulation structure.

The cepstrum presents periodic spectral-spacing structure.

When compatible relationships appear in these different representations, confidence in the interpretation can increase.

Correlating the Evidence

This is the central purpose of the exercise.

The analytical progression can be thought of as:

                 ORIGINAL SIGNAL
                       │
        ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
        ▼              ▼              ▼
      LOFAR           DEMON        CEPSTRUM
        │              │              │
        ▼              ▼              ▼
    Spectral       Modulation       Regular
    structure        rates          spacing
        │              │              │
        └──────────────┼──────────────┘
                       ▼
               CORRELATED EVIDENCE
                       │
                       ▼
             Physical interpretation


No individual display needs to carry the whole interpretation.

LOFAR may reveal persistent spectral features but leave their origin uncertain. DEMON may expose a family of modulation rates. The cepstrum may independently indicate corresponding periodic spacing. Mathematical relationships can then test whether these observations form a physically coherent pattern.

This layered confirmation principle is explicitly identified in 0_2_5 AI Review Synthesis.txt as the means by which an analyst avoids over-interpreting one attractive spectral peak.

Summary of Findings

The synthetic example begins with a known:

SR=1.30 Hz\boxed{SR=1.30\text{ Hz}}

Shaft frequency converts directly to rotational speed:

RPM=60SRRPM=60SR

therefore:

RPM=60×1.30RPM=60\times1.30 RPM=78\boxed{RPM=78}

For five propeller blades:

BR=B×SRBR=B\times SR

and therefore:

BR=5×1.30BR=5\times1.30 BR=6.50 Hz\boxed{BR=6.50\text{ Hz}}

The next Blade Rate harmonic occurs at:

2BR=2×6.502BR=2\times6.50 2BR=13.00 Hz\boxed{2BR=13.00\text{ Hz}}

Conversely, if SR and BR were measured without knowing the blade count:

B=BRSRB=\frac{BR}{SR} B=6.501.30B=\frac{6.50}{1.30} B=5\boxed{B=5}

Finally, the corresponding reciprocal relationships provide expected cepstral quefrencies:

qBR=16.50≈0.154 sq_{BR}=\frac{1}{6.50}\approx\boxed{0.154\text{ s}}

and:

qSR=11.30≈0.769 sq_{SR}=\frac{1}{1.30}\approx\boxed{0.769\text{ s}}

The exercise therefore brings the analysis full circle. LOFAR identifies spectral structure, DEMON exposes modulation structure, and the cepstrum searches for periodic spectral spacing. The mathematical relationships between Shaft Rate, rotational speed, Blade Rate, harmonics and quefrency then provide a common framework within which those observations can be compared.

The important finding is consequently not simply “there is a peak at 6.5 Hz”. It is the much stronger chain of reasoning:

1.30 Hz→78 RPM→5×1.30→6.50 Hz→2×6.50→13.00 Hz\boxed{ 1.30\text{ Hz} \rightarrow 78\text{ RPM} \rightarrow 5\times1.30 \rightarrow 6.50\text{ Hz} \rightarrow 2\times6.50 \rightarrow 13.00\text{ Hz} }

supported by complementary views of the same underlying signal.

That is ultimately what the exercise demonstrates: acoustic analysis becomes most persuasive when observations from different analytical domains agree with one another and with the underlying mathematics, rather than when an interpretation depends upon a single conspicuous peak. This reflects the broader MACS principle that the physics should support the interpretation.

Why there are so many cepstral peaks

The real cepstrum is calculated from the logarithm of the complete signal spectrum:

c(q)=Re⁡{F−1[log⁡∣F{x(t)}∣]}c(q)=\operatorname{Re}\left\{ \mathcal{F}^{-1} \left[ \log\left|\mathcal{F}\{x(t)\}\right| \right] \right\}

It responds to any reasonably regular spacing in the spectrum, not specifically to Shaft Rate or Blade Rate.

The synthetic recording contains:

several carrier frequencies;
machinery tonals;
modulation sidebands;
harmonics;
broadband noise;
finite-window effects;
interactions between unrelated components.

Each spacing can generate its own cepstral structure. For example, spectral lines separated by 2 Hz can contribute around 0.5 s, while lines separated by 10 Hz can contribute around 0.1 s. The resulting cepstrum can therefore contain many peaks, only some of which relate to propulsion periodicities.

What the cepstrum actually establishes

A peak at 0.154 s does not directly mean “Blade Rate”. It means that the spectrum contains evidence of repeated spacing near:

Δf=10.154≈6.5 Hz\Delta f=\frac{1}{0.154}\approx6.5\ \text{Hz}

Similarly, a peak at 0.769 s indicates possible spectral spacing near:

Δf=10.769≈1.3 Hz\Delta f=\frac{1}{0.769}\approx1.3\ \text{Hz}

The mechanical labels are assigned only after comparing those spacings with evidence from the other analyses.

That distinction is central:

Cepstral peak
      ↓
Candidate spectral spacing
      ↓
Compare with DEMON and LOFAR
      ↓
Test harmonic and blade relationships
      ↓
Possible SR or BR interpretation


The cepstrum should therefore be used as corroborating evidence, not as an independent SR/BR detector. This agrees with the layered-confirmation approach in 0_2_5 AI Review Synthesis.txt, which warns against identifying machinery from one attractive peak and instead recommends seeking independent evidence.

How an analyst would identify the relevant peaks
1. Begin with DEMON, not the cepstrum

In this exercise, the mean DEMON spectrum provides the clearest evidence of modulation near:

SR≈1.30 HzSR\approx1.30\ \text{Hz}

and:

BR≈6.50 HzBR\approx6.50\ \text{Hz}

Their ratio is:

BRSR=6.501.30=5\frac{BR}{SR} = \frac{6.50}{1.30} = 5

This supports a five-bladed propeller interpretation.

The DEMON result therefore gives the analyst two candidate periodicities to test elsewhere.

2. Convert those candidates to quefrency

Once 1.30 Hz and 6.50 Hz have been identified independently, calculate:

qSR=11.30=0.769 sq_{SR}=\frac{1}{1.30}=0.769\ \text{s}

and:

qBR=16.50=0.154 sq_{BR}=\frac{1}{6.50}=0.154\ \text{s}

The cepstrum is then inspected around those positions.

This is a targeted test of a prior hypothesis, not an unrestricted search for the largest cepstral peaks.

3. Examine a tolerance region

Because the synthetic shaft speed varies slightly, neither SR nor BR is perfectly stationary. Windowing and finite frequency resolution also broaden the response.

The analysis should therefore examine a region rather than demand a peak at one exact sample. For example:

SR search region: approximately 0.74–0.80 s
BR search region: approximately 0.145–0.165 s

These ranges are suggested inspection regions for this particular teaching signal, not universal detection thresholds.

4. Require a local maximum above a local background

A credible candidate should be a local maximum relative to nearby quefrencies. Merely having some amplitude at 0.154 or 0.769 s is insufficient.

A useful display can compare the candidate with a local baseline:

P=c(q)−median⁡(clocal)MAD⁡(clocal)P=\frac{c(q)-\operatorname{median}(c_{\text{local}})} {\operatorname{MAD}(c_{\text{local}})}

where MAD is the median absolute deviation.

This expresses how far the candidate rises above its local cepstral background. It is preferable to selecting the largest peak across the entire plot because cepstral amplitude can vary considerably with quefrency.

5. Look for coherent families

A single cepstral peak is weak evidence. A coherent family is more useful.

For Blade Rate, possible related quefrencies include:

qBR=1BR=0.154 sq_{BR}=\frac{1}{BR}=0.154\ \text{s} 2qBR=0.308 s2q_{BR}=0.308\ \text{s} 3qBR=0.462 s3q_{BR}=0.462\ \text{s}

These do not have to appear strongly, but repeated structure related to the same fundamental spacing provides more support than one isolated feature.

Care is required here because harmonics in the frequency domain and repeated peaks in the quefrency domain are not interchangeable without considering how the signal was formed.

A stronger analysis method

For the MACS exercise, I would use the following evidence chain:

LOFAR identifies the carrier frequencies, persistent tonal structure and possible sidebands.
DEMON estimates candidate modulation rates near 1.30 and 6.50 Hz.
The ratio 6.50/1.30=56.50/1.30=5 supports a five-bladed relationship.
Cepstral analysis tests whether the spectrum contains repeated spacing compatible with those independently estimated rates.
A cepstral candidate is accepted only if a local peak exists near the expected quefrency and rises meaningfully above its local background.

The inference is therefore:

DEMON evidence→candidate rate→reciprocal quefrency→cepstral corroboration\text{DEMON evidence} \rightarrow \text{candidate rate} \rightarrow \text{reciprocal quefrency} \rightarrow \text{cepstral corroboration}

not:

largest cepstral peak→automatic machinery identification\text{largest cepstral peak} \rightarrow \text{automatic machinery identification}
